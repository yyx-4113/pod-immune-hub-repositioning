# A two-axis framework for postoperative delirium: APOE ε4 susceptibility and a candidate peripheral immune state axis

**Yongxin Yang**^1^✉

^1^ Department of Anesthesiology, The Second Affiliated Hospital of Fujian University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Fuzhou, Fujian 350003, China

✉ Corresponding author: 960856791@qq.com | ORCID: 0009-0004-9698-6552

## Abstract

Postoperative delirium (POD) is common and morbid; a peripheral innate-immune state is hypothesised but untested against APOE ε4 susceptibility. We integrated four public omics layers: a blood transcriptome (n = 8), paired PBMC scRNA-seq (GSE252572), paired blood methylation, 65 POD cases (GSE330869), and a POD GWAS (1,016 / 139,148), with an in-silico LINCS L1000 screen (pre-specified positive controls; cell composition as covariate). Immune genes were directionally elevated in POD blood (p = 0.0079). The GWAS was APOE ε4-driven (OR 1.86 per allele; 37 significant variants, all chromosome 19); excluding chromosome 19, the immune gene set was null (p = 0.277), and no small molecule cleared FDR (11 of 24 ligand hits net reversals; glucocorticoid marginal positive-control pass, one-sided p = 0.041). Composition adjustment removed 50.4% of differentially methylated regions (13,357 → 6,628) and abolished the immune-specific enrichment (p = 2.46 × 10⁻⁴ → 0.72). We propose, but do not demonstrate, a two-axis model: a constitutive APOE ε4 susceptibility axis plus a candidate peripheral immune state axis whose independence from APOE ε4 is untested. The state axis is a candidate therapeutic target whose modifiability is unproven; the screen supplies hypotheses, not efficacy evidence.

**Keywords:** postoperative delirium; APOE ε4; innate immunity; multi-omics; LINCS L1000; genome-wide association study

## Background

Postoperative delirium (POD) affects 10–50% of older surgical patients and is associated with longer hospital stays, cognitive decline, and mortality [1,29,30,39]. POD (acute, days) is clinically distinct from postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD, subacute/chronic, weeks–months) [41]; this paper concerns POD, and POCD/PND are cited only as best-available proxies where POD-specific evidence is absent. Despite its prevalence, mechanism-guided prevention is lacking [30]. Two non-exclusive lines of evidence dominate the literature. First, a state of innate-immune activation (peripheral leukocyte transcriptional reprogramming, cytokine release, and blood–brain barrier disruption) has been repeatedly observed around the time of surgery [2,19,36], and neuroinflammation is now viewed as a core, potentially tractable POD/PND mechanism [35], in line with the neuroinflammatory hypothesis of delirium [42,43] and its framing as a systemic inflammatory brain response in older adults [44]. Blood–brain barrier breakdown accompanying surgery correlates with delirium and with cerebrospinal-fluid inflammatory and metabolic changes [21,37]; peripheral damage-associated molecular patterns such as HMGB1 can activate microglia and propagate this signal to the brain [45]. Second, the APOE ε4 allele, the principal genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's disease, is a replicated epidemiological risk factor for short-/medium-term postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) [3] (the same meta-analysis reported a non-significant POD subgroup association: OR 1.28, 95% CI 0.85–1.91, p = 0.23), and in the largest POD-specific GWAS to date APOE ε4 is the dominant genetic signal for POD [7]; APOE isoforms shape the human glial transcriptome in aging and Alzheimer's disease [25], and astrocyte-derived APOE4 impairs the blood–brain barrier [28]. A two-hit framework is increasingly used to organise these observations: a pre-existing vulnerability (e.g. APOE ε4, ageing) plus a perioperative second hit (surgery, anaesthesia, innate-immune activation) [27,40]. Peripheral immune signals reach the brain through multiple routes, including leucocyte subset shifts and cytokine transport [36]; purinergic signalling through adenosine receptors modulates microglial and neuroinflammatory responses [20,23,24]; and the kynurenine pathway has also been implicated [31]. Consistent with a peripheral leucocyte contribution, preoperative and early postoperative neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio and other inflammatory markers have been associated with POD [18,33,34]; noting that [18] is a mixed critical-illness meta-analysis whose surgical-POD subgroup estimate (WMD 1.14) is much weaker than its ICU subgroup (4.22), limiting direct inference to elective surgical POD.

What has been missing is a framework that places these two observations in the same causal graph. Are they the same axis (APOE ε4 acts *through* immune activation), or two independent axes (constitutive susceptibility plus a separately surgery-contextual immune state)? Resolving this matters for therapeutics: if they are one axis, immune-modulating drugs would merely track a fixed genotype; if they are two axes, immune modulation becomes a candidate, interventionally testable target (candidate-modifiable in the sense of being unfixed by genotype, not established as modifiable by evidence of efficacy). We use 'peripheral immune activation state axis' as a working, integrative construct spanning directional blood-transcriptomic immune upregulation, a largely leucocyte-composition-associated perioperative methylation shift, and in-silico immune-signature reversibility, not a single measured endpoint; each component is hypothesis-generating. Operationally, we define the primary, measurable endpoint of the state axis as post-operative upregulation of the blood interferon/innate-immune transcriptional program (Layer B), acknowledging that its peri-operative triggering is inferred, not directly measured, because Layer B is a static post-operative case-control; the leucocyte-composition-associated methylation shift (Layer A) and the LINCS in-silico reversibility (Layer D) are corroborating, not definitional, evidence. Falsification of the state axis requires the absence of this primary transcriptional signal in an independent surgical cohort and the absence of a composition-independent immune-methylation signal after leucocyte adjustment.

We addressed this using a multi-omics integration strategy anchored on publicly available POD datasets, an in-silico drug-repositioning screen against the LINCS L1000 resource (chemical and virtual-knockout arms), and genetic confirmation from a large POD GWAS. Critically, we fixed our analytic decisions and positive controls *a priori* (documented in the version-controlled analysis repository, with its commit history) rather than after seeing results, and we report null and negative findings without rescue. No formal registry entry (e.g. OSF) was created, and we therefore avoid the term "pre-registered" in its formal sense.

### Pre/post-operative blood methylation (Layer A)

GSE330869 provides 65 POD cases each with paired pre- and post-operative peripheral-blood EPIC v2 methylation profiles (130 samples); GEO deposition contains POD cases only (no non-delirium controls), so the analysable signal is the surgery-induced shift *within* POD. This POD-only deposition was verified by NCBI E-utilities and GEO FTP; Seki 2026's published Time×Group methylation anchors (e.g. cg01534316→TMIGD3/ADORA3, FDR = 0.021) derive from a Time×Group methylation model on the full cohort including non-delirium controls (Seki 2026, Methods) that were not deposited to GEO. cg01534316_TC21 (a Type-I EPIC-v2 probe not present in the standard v2.0 manifest, as reported by Seki 2026), so the published anchor attribution is unaffected by the deposition design. We recomputed this layer in-house (R/Bioconductor). Cell composition was estimated by reference-based deconvolution (Houseman 2012 [10]; EpiDISH centCAB100i centroids [12]; CIBERSORT as a cross-check [13]) and entered as a covariate in the sensitivity analyses below. Autosomal, gene-mapped probes (n = 312,514) were taken forward. Paired differential methylation used limma empirical-Bayes moderation with patient as a blocking factor; a differential methylation probe (DMP) required FDR < 0.05 and |Δβ| > 0.05. Two immune gene sets are used in this paper and should not be conflated. **Set 1** (Layer A, methylation, directional probe test): a curated NF-κB/leukocyte set of 49 genes, contributing 737 probes on the EPIC v2 array retained here. **Set 2** (Layer B, transcriptome; also used for DMR gene-body mapping and for the composition-adjusted probe analysis): a broader 83-gene innate-immune set (see Layer B), contributing 1,195 probes on the retained EPIC v2 array. To test whether the immune axis is reflected in methylation beyond the few genome-wide DMPs, we performed a *directional* probe-level test mirroring Layer B: a two-sided Mann–Whitney U test on the distribution of within-POD pre→post limma t-statistics of Set-1 immune-gene probes (n = 737) versus all background probes (the direction is read from the sign of the statistic, not from a one-tailed test). An elastic-net within-subject methylation autoregression (WMA; glmnet, α = 0.5, 10-fold CV) related pre-operative immune-probe M-values to post-operative immune-probe M-values (cross-validated R² reported strictly as a within-subject retest descriptor of immune-methylation profile stability across surgery, **not** as a methylation risk score predicting a phenotype, which the within-POD paired design here cannot supply). Differentially methylated regions (DMRs) were called with **bumphunter** on the M-value scale (|ΔM| ≥ 0.05, length ≥ 3 probes); bumphunter was run with **B = 0** (no permutation), so DMR counts are descriptive without empirical FDR control and should not be read as controlled counts. Two distinct composition-adjustment covariate sets were used and must not be conflated: (a) at the **probe level**, paired ΔM was regressed on the estimated change in neutrophils, lymphocytes, and monocytes (the classic three-lineage covariate); (b) at the **DMR level**, the bumphunter design additionally entered neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils (granulocyte-subtype covariates, since eosinophils and basophils are sub-lineages rather than independent lineages). The two covariate sets therefore probe different biology and yield different attenuation magnitudes (see Results). Two caveats are material: (i) Day0/Day3 time points are not encoded in the GEO matrix header, so the WMA uses all post-operative profiles rather than a Day0-only baseline subgroup; (ii) DMR calling was performed with **bumphunter** (minfi/Bioconductor, loadable here) rather than DMRcate, because DMRcate's compiled dependency segfaults on load under R 4.4.3 / ucrt and this environment has no Rtools to recompile it, so bumphunter was run with **B = 0 (no permutation)**, so its DMRs are *descriptive* (no empirical FDR) and are used as a supportive regional view, not a genome-wide claim; (iii) cell-type composition was estimated by reference-based deconvolution implemented in **Python**, using the reference centroids bundled inside the already-installed `EpiDISH` package (`centBloodSub.m` 188x7, `centDHSbloodDMC.m` 333x7, `cent12CT.m` and `cent12CT450k.m` 600x12, and `centCAB100i.m` 1906x19). This route requires neither the platform manifest nor network access, so it is reproducible wherever `EpiDISH` and numpy/scipy/scikit-learn are present, and it supersedes the earlier manifest-dependent plan. Reference matrices were exported to text and solved as a constrained linear mixture on beta values (Houseman et al. 2012 [10]; solver comparison as in Teschendorff et al. 2017 [11]) (w_k >= 0, sum w_k = 1) by four solvers - NNLS with an explicit sum-to-one penalty, NNLS with post-hoc normalisation, linear nu-SVR (CIBERSORT-style), and Tukey-biweight iteratively reweighted least squares (RPC-style); all four agreed within ~1 percentage point for the dominant lineages. Because the deposited beta matrix retains only 312,514 rows (~33% of EPIC v2 probes), each reference contributed only 66-641 informative probes after intersection. **This restricted row coverage - not any probe-identifier mismatch - is the binding constraint**, which is why five independent references were run rather than one. Fit was high throughout (mean R2 = 0.90-0.97; Pearson r = 0.95-0.98 between observed and mixture-predicted beta). Subtype-level fractions (e.g. CD4T naive vs memory, Treg) are **not identifiable** owing to strong collinearity among lymphoid centroids; only lineage-level estimates (granulocyte/neutrophil, total lymphoid, monocyte) are interpreted. (iv) To test whether the pre->post immune methylation signal is independent of the estimated composition shift, each probe's paired delta-M (n = 65 subjects) was regressed on the subject-level change in estimated neutrophil fraction, and in a second model jointly on the changes in neutrophil, total-lymphoid and monocyte fractions; the intercept t-statistics from these models were then fed into the same immune-versus-background competitive test used for the unadjusted analysis. At the DMR level the same covariates were entered into the bumphunter design: because the model was fitted on per-subject paired M-value differences, subject effects cancel exactly, so the intercept estimates the composition-adjusted mean change without subject dummy variables; covariates were the per-subject change in estimated neutrophil fraction, and in a second model the neutrophil, eosinophil and basophil changes jointly (total-lymphoid and monocyte changes were not entered together with the neutrophil change because the closed composition makes them nearly dependent). DMRs were called with the same settings throughout (|coefficient| >= 0.05, length >= 3 probes, B = 0).

Differentially methylated regions were called with bumphunter using **B = 0 (no bootstrap permutation)**, so DMR counts are descriptive and carry no empirical FDR control; they are reported as hypothesis-generating regional context only. Paired neutrophil estimates are taken from `analysis/layerA_deconv_summary.csv` (rows where celltype = aNeu/Neutro and config = centCAB100i_a12), with per-sample fractions in `analysis/layerA_cell_proportions_python.csv`. (The file `analysis/results/GSE330869/layerA_cell_proportions.csv` is a degenerate centBloodSub NNLS output (Neu/CD4T collapsed to 0) and is **not** the source of the reported neutrophil Δ, which is reproduced from `layerA_deconv_summary.csv`.) Immune-gene overlap of each DMR was mapped probe-to-gene using the IlluminaHumanMethylationEPICv2anno.20a1.hg38 annotation (hg38 build) and the 83-gene Set-2 immune list (`analysis/signatures/immune_genes.txt`); per-DMR overlap tables are committed as `bumphunter_DMR_{unadjusted,adj_dNeu,adj_dNeu_Eos_Baso}_immune_overlap.csv` (R script `analysis/layerA_dmr_immune_overlap_provenance.R`, `set.seed(12345)`), providing traceable counts of **79 / 24 / 36 / 34 immune-overlapping DMRs** (unadjusted / adj_dNeu / adj_dNeu_Eos_Baso / **adj_3lineage**, the last being the spec behind the 50.4% headline; these are region counts, not gene counts; because multiple DMRs can overlap the same gene, the distinct immune-gene counts from these same files are 48 / 21 / 29 / 27, the 34/27 pair carrying the 50.4% reduction 13,357 → 6,628). The stricter Set-2 probe→gene mapping in `bumphunter_adjusted_summary.csv` reports **48 / 21 / 29 hit immune genes**; a broader probe→gene annotation in `layerA_DMR_immune_gene_counts.csv` gives 75 hit genes: the 48/75 gene-count spread (distinct from the separate DMR-region counts 79 / 24 / 36) reflects different mapping rules, not a numerical error, and only 48/21/29 are reported in the Results.

### Transcriptomic immune gene-set enrichment (Layer B)

Raw GSE163943 was processed with limma empirical Bayes moderation (44,824 probes tested; 4,146 at nominal p < 0.05 without fold-change cutoff; n_up = 737, n_down = 996 at nominal p < 0.05 **and** |log2FC| ≥ 0.585 (1.5-fold); 0 at FDR < 0.05, reflecting the n = 8 sample size). To test whether immune genes were *directionally* shifted, we defined an immune gene set (n = 83 genes present on the array) and compared the distribution of moderated t-statistics of immune genes versus the 44,741 background genes with a two-sided Mann–Whitney U test. An unweighted one-sample t-test on immune-gene t-values tested mean non-zero shift; this is conservative when per-gene variances are heterogeneous (as is the case here, n = 4 v 4), so the resulting p-value is not a bound on the directional effect size and should not be read as a lower limit on the signal. The 48 up- and 35 down-regulated immune genes (nominal p < 0.05 and |log2FC| ≥ 0.585; 47/34 after gene-symbol reconciliation) were carried forward as the immune-restricted disease signature for LINCS; only the subset of these genes with non-empty LINCS L1000 consensus signatures enters the overlap enrichment, so the effective screenable signature is materially smaller than the 83-gene input set.

### Single-cell PBMC localisation (Layer C)

Seven usable 10x libraries from GSE252572 were filtered (QC), log-normalised (CPM + log1p), passed to highly-variable-gene selection, SVD, and K-means (k = 12). **Crucially, clustering was performed on the pooled 52,912-cell matrix, not per-sample**, so that cluster identities are comparable across samples (a per-sample clustering error was detected and corrected during analysis). Clusters were annotated by classic marker scoring. Cell-type proportions were compared across POD status at the *sample* level (Mann–Whitney U) to avoid pseudoreplication.

### In-silico immune-axis screening (Layer D)

Drug and CRISPR-knockout perturbation signatures were the LINCS L1000 consensus gene sets [8,9,32] distributed as Enrichr libraries (chemical up/down, ligand up/down, CRISPR-KO consensus, GEO-based). Disease signature DisUp (up in POD) and DisDown (down in POD). Reversal of a perturbation = its down-set enriched for DisUp **and/or** its up-set enriched for DisDown. Strength was scored by Fisher-exact overlap (one-sided, greater) against the LINCS gene universe, combined by Fisher's method (χ², 4 df), with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across all signatures. An empirical size-matched null (3,000 permutations) yielded z-scores and empirical p-values.

**Chemical arm.** Perturbagens = small molecules (the Enrichr *chemical* library, n = 1,069) **and** cytokine *ligand* perturbations (the Enrichr *ligand* library, n = 96). These two sub-libraries are analysed separately below and must not be conflated: the term "drug-level FDR hits" refers to whichever sub-library is named, and the small-molecule chemical arm and the cytokine ligand arm have distinct hit counts.

**Virtual-knockout arm.** CRISPR-KO consensus terms are "GENE Up" / "GENE Down" (~240 genes each). Knockout of a driver gene should reverse the disease signature exactly like a therapeutic perturbation; we therefore scored each gene's KO exactly as a chemical perturbation.

**A-priori (pre-specified) positive controls.** Glucocorticoids and immunosuppressants (dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, budesonide, cyclosporine, sirolimus, tacrolimus, statins, NSAIDs, …) must pass the class-level permutation test (one-sided p < 0.05, matching the pre-specified reversal direction) and/or rank individual drugs in the top 10% (≥ 90th percentile); if neither, the screen is reported as failed. (The pre-specified top-percentile single-drug criterion was met on the whole-transcriptome arm but not on the immune-restricted arm, where the class-level criterion was met instead (see Results). For the KO arm, knockouts of known pro-inflammatory drivers (NFKB1, RELA, STAT3, IL1RAP, NFKBIZ, IL6, …) were the positive-control class.

### Genetic context and consistency check (Layer F)

The POD GWAS (12,353,257 variants; genomic-control λ = 1.0133) was scanned for the APOE ε4-defining alleles. The ε4 isoform is jointly defined by rs429358-C and rs7412-C; rs429358-C is a near-perfect tag for the ε4 haplotype in European-ancestry UKB data (ε4 requires rs429358-C with rs7412-C; rs7412 is not in the released top-loci and was not independently verified here) and was used here as the lead variant. Because the POD GWAS is monolithic on chromosome 19 (37 genome-wide-significant SNPs, all APOE block), **no immune-trait→POD Mendelian randomization and no immune PRS are feasible**: POD instruments exist only at APOE, and no immune gene reaches genome-wide significance. This layer is therefore an association and gene-set analysis, not an MR, and provides no causal evidence for either axis; it is reported as a consistency check, not as confirmation. We then tested the immune-gene set with a stratified permutation (5,000 permutations, matched on SNP count per gene to control gene-length confounding); gene density (SNP/kb, a proxy for mappability and GC content) was not separately controlled, so immune genes in unusually dense or sparse regions may retain residual confounding. The permutation was computed both genome-wide and after excluding chromosome 19 (which carries APOE and its LD neighbours).

### Docking audit (Layer E)

Each anchor protein was queried against the PDB for experimentally determined structures; genes without an experimental structure were flagged as requiring an AlphaFold model or exclusion from docking.

### A-priori specification and honesty posture

Primary hypotheses and the positive-control success criterion were fixed before unblinding results. The originally planned "hub-gene → POD Mendelian randomization" was specified a priori as expected-null given APOE-driven GWAS architecture and was not pursued as a positive claim. All null and negative results are reported.

## Results

### Immune gene expression is directionally elevated in POD blood (Layer B)

Across 83 annotated immune genes on the GSE163943 array, moderated t-statistics were shifted toward upregulation relative to the 44,741 background genes (mean t = 0.253 versus −0.171; Mann–Whitney U p = **0.0079**), while an unweighted one-sample test for mean non-zero shift was non-significant (p = 0.18); the mean per-gene shift is small relative to heterogeneous per-gene variances at n = 4 v 4, so the signal is broad and directional rather than driven by a few large per-gene effects, At nominal p < 0.05, 737 transcripts were up- and 996 down-regulated (0 at FDR < 0.05, as expected for n = 8). The 48 up- and 35 down-regulated immune genes defined the immune-restricted signature carried into LINCS.

### No cell-type proportion shift in PBMC, and methylation anchors are absent from blood (Layer C)

Global clustering of 52,912 PBMC cells yielded 12 stable clusters. Cell-type proportions did not differ by POD status at the sample level; at the patient level (2 vs 2) the minimum achievable one-sided Mann–Whitney p is exactly 1/6 ≈ 0.167 (the library-level 4-vs-3 floor is 1/35 ≈ 0.029, while the observed most-extreme p is 0.229), so this proportion test is descriptive and the p-value is read as a floor, not biological evidence of no shift; e.g. monocyte 6.6% POD vs 18.2% control, NK 40.4% vs 23.0%). Within-subject pre/post variability (e.g. monocyte 25.0% → 8.1%) exceeded between-group differences, so Layer C is treated as descriptive localisation only.

A finding with interpretive weight: the methylation anchors **TMIGD3** (mean expression 0–0.0023) and **ADORA3** (0.0002–0.0015) are essentially unexpressed in PBMC, whereas CD63 (0.46–0.71) and STAT3 (0.53–0.68) are strongly expressed. Peripheral blood is therefore not the tissue in which to validate these epigenetic loci, consistent with the two-axis view that the constitutive (methylation/APOE) axis is not simply read out in circulating immune cells. **This null is not in conflict with the composition shift detected by deconvolution of the whole-blood methylome (below):** Layer C assays PBMC, in which granulocytes are depleted by preparation, so a granulocyte/neutrophil shift is largely invisible to it, and it comprises 2 POD vs 2 non-POD patients (4 vs 3 usable libraries) versus 65 in Layer A, giving it far less power to detect any shift.

### Epigenetic pre→post immune shift within POD (Layer A)

Recomputed in-house (n = 312,514 autosomal gene-mapped EPIC v2 probes; 65 paired POD cases), the within-POD pre→post methylome shift is small: only 6 DMPs survived FDR < 0.05 and |Δβ| > 0.05 (0 at |Δβ| > 0.10; |Δβ| range 0.05–0.065), and none mapped to a curated immune gene (0/49 overlap; Fisher p = 1). Thus individual immune loci are not genome-wide significant in this paired-within-POD design. However, at the probe-set level the immune axis *is* directionally present: immune-gene probes showed a significant pre→post shift relative to background (one-sided MWU, alternative = greater [immune t > background], on limma moderated-t statistics, Set-1, 49-gene/737-probe set, unadjusted, p = 0.041; two-sided ~ 0.082; mean t = +0.064 immune vs −0.035 background); this is the unadjusted Set-1 test and has no composition-adjusted analogue using the same limma moderated-t definition (its only adjusted sibling is a different Set-2 OLS-on-delta test, null at 0.296/0.098/0.141; see companion table below), and it is reported as hypothesis-generating and not yet composition-validated (see Limitations and §Composition-adjustment below); it is directionally consistent with Seki 2026's NF-κB/leukocyte pathway finding (Seki used a Time×Group model with non-delirium controls; our within-POD design has no controls and is therefore not a strict replication). The immune-methylation profile was individually stable across surgery (WMA within-subject retest R² = 0.79 (in-sample after λ selection by 10-fold cross-validation), a within-subject retest descriptor of profile stability rather than evidence of axis stability); the small state-dependent shift is superimposed on a largely constitutive immune-methylation baseline. (Note: the immune-probe count n = 737 here is a coincidence with the Layer B n_up = 737 transcript count; the two numbers refer to unrelated objects: methylation probes versus transcriptome probes.) At the regional level, **bumphunter** (run with B = 0, no permutation; descriptive counts without empirical FDR, see Methods) called **13,357 DMRs (descriptive, B = 0)** (|ΔM| ≥ 0.05 on the M-value scale and length ≥ 3 probes) across the 65 paired POD cases; of these, 79 overlapped an immune gene unadjusted, falling to 24 (neutrophil-adjusted) and 36 (neutrophil + eosinophil + basophil-adjusted), with 48, 21 and 29 immune genes hit respectively (per-DMR gene lists committed as `bumphunter_DMR_{unadjusted,adj_dNeu,adj_dNeu_Eos_Baso}_immune_overlap.csv`; of the 48 immune genes hit unadjusted, 14 remained hit after neutrophil adjustment, per `bumphunter_immune_gene_before_after.csv`). The broader 73,825 candidate regions were near-balanced (37,339 hyper- vs 36,486 hypo-methylated), whereas the stricter 13,357 DMRs were hypo-methylation dominated (4,994 vs 8,363), again arguing against a uniform global hyper-methylation sweep. Mapping these strict DMR intervals to gene bodies showed **79 DMRs (0.59%) overlapping at least one of the 83 curated immune genes, hitting 48/83 immune genes (4 of the 83 immune-set members had no mapped probes, so the effective denominator is 79)**; the most frequently hit were IL6R (4 DMRs), TNF (4), CX3CR1 (3), IRF8 (3), S100A8 (3) and TNFAIP3 (3) - again the TNF / NF-kappaB / IL-1 axis. (On the broader candidate-region set *before* the length filter, 305/73,825 regions (0.41%) and 75/83 genes overlapped, with TNFAIP3 the most hit at 12; we report the length-filtered set here because that is the DMR definition used throughout.) We then re-ran bumphunter with the estimated per-subject change in cell fractions entered as covariates (Methods (iv)). Entering the estimated neutrophil change alone cut the DMR burden from 13,357 to 5,153 (-61%) and the immune-overlapping DMRs from 79 to 24 (-70%), leaving 21/83 immune genes hit and only 14 of the original 48 genes still represented; additionally adjusting for eosinophil and basophil changes gave an intermediate result (7,263 DMRs; 36 immune-overlapping). Because those two ends come from *different* covariate specifications rather than from one sensitivity envelope, we also re-ran the DMR model with the **same three-lineage covariate set used at the probe level** (neutrophil + lymphoid + monocyte): this removed 50.4% of DMRs (13,357 → 6,628) and left 34 immune-overlapping DMRs hitting 27/83 immune genes, with 21 of the original 48 genes still represented (`bumphunter_3lineage_summary.csv`, `bumphunter_DMR_adj_3lineage_immune_overlap.csv`; the unadjusted model was re-run in the same script and reproduced 13,357 / 79 / 48). Critically, the *proportion* of immune-overlapping DMRs did not rise under any specification (0.59% unadjusted → 0.47% neutrophil-only, 0.50% granulocyte-subtype, **0.51% three-lineage**), so what composition adjustment removes is a **global** post-surgical methylation shift rather than a disproportionally immune component. The regional immune signal is therefore largely - though not entirely - attributable to the post-operative leucocyte composition shift; because bumphunter ran at B = 0, all DMR counts and every percentage derived from them are descriptive, not inferential attributions, and the single-specification estimate of the composition-attributable share is ~50%. (Because bumphunter ran without permutation, these DMR counts are descriptive, not FDR-controlled.) It is important to state what this analysis does *not* show. It does not show that Layer A is uninformative: the composition shift itself (neutrophils +3.4 pp, p = 7.6 × 10⁻⁴, consistent across five references) is a positive, reproducible finding, and the methylation change that accompanies it is observed (13,357 DMRs; directional immune shift p = 0.041, unadjusted). What it shows is that these two are largely the same phenomenon rather than two independent ones. Read this way, the methylation layer is still consistent with the two-axis hypothesis, but with a material qualification: what surgery triggers here is largely a **leucocyte-composition shift together with its accompanying methylation change**, over and above which lies only a modest residual component that composition adjustment does not remove. Neither component is constitutive, and both remain dissociable from the APOE ε4 susceptibility axis; we therefore do not claim that Layer A demonstrates cell-intrinsic innate-immune epigenetic reprogramming.

### LINCS in-silico screening: chemical arm partially supportive, virtual-knockout arm hypothesis-generating (Layer D)

All LINCS perturbation signatures are consensus profiles from a heterogeneous panel of cancer cell lines (dominated by non-breast lines such as HA1E, VCAP, PC3, HCC515, A375, A549, HT29, with MCF7 among them), not neuroimmune or leucocyte models; reversal of a human POD-blood signature by these profiles is therefore hypothesis-generating, cell-line-remote evidence and should not be read as human efficacy. **Chemical arm.** Across 1,069 perturbagens, the pre-specified positive-control class test was marginal at the **class level** on the immune-restricted signature: the glucocorticoid class ranked above the size-matched null (class mean 58.46% vs null 49.92 ± 4.87%, z_class = 1.75, one-sided class permutation p = 0.041, which meets the pre-specified one-sided p < 0.05 bar only marginally (two-sided p = 0.081; the whole-transcriptome arm p = 0.056 also exceeds 0.05), so the glucocorticoid class is a weak positive-control pass rather than a definitive one). The whole-transcriptome arm was non-significant (class p = 0.056), so this is a directional, class-level signal rather than a definitive positive-control pass. The glucocorticoid class bundles systemic and topical/dermatologic agents (≥13 topical steroids, none usable as CNS/perioperative POD preventives), so this marginal pass reflects a broad anti-inflammatory transcriptional reversal rather than a glucocorticoid-class repositioning lead and should not be read as one. The high per-drug percentiles (dexamethasone 95.1%, hydrocortisone 92.0%, betamethasone 90.9%, prednisolone 87.2%; per-drug generic-name percentiles from the summary JSON) belong to the **whole-transcriptome arm**, not the immune-restricted arm; on the immune-restricted arm the same drugs' percentiles were dexamethasone acetate 76.6%, hydrocortisone 79.4%, betamethasone 27.4%, prednisolone 76.3% (per-formulation values from the class-test members list; the summary-JSON reports higher generic-name chemical-arm percentiles that aggregate all formulations and are a different quantity), so the class-level pass is not driven by top-percentile single-drug ranking but by the class as a whole. Two pre-specified positive-control classes: statins (immune-restricted perm p = 0.85; whole-transcriptome p = 0.80) and NSAIDs (p = 0.78; 0.93) did **not** reverse the signature; per our a priori rule these are *failed* positive controls and temper confidence in the screen's sensitivity, and we report the screen as hypothesis-generating, not as having fully passed its positive-control battery. On the immune-restricted signature, **six IFNγ perturbations (across MCF7, MCF10A, HS578T, MDAMB231, BT20, SKBR3) all reversed the disease signature at FDR < 0.05**; the cited value FDR = 3.4 × 10⁻⁴ corresponds to ifng-mdamb231, which is the **second-strongest** of the six (strongest: ifng-mcf7 FDR = 1.9 × 10⁻⁴; weakest: ifng-hs578t FDR = 2.5 × 10⁻²). Because the disease signature is enriched for interferon-response and innate-immune genes (IFNG and CXCL10 are among the 48 input up-genes), this reversal is partly tautological across all six entries and is reported as hypothesis-generating rather than a directional mechanistic lead. **At the drug level, 24 of 96 LINCS *ligand* (cytokine) perturbations survived BH-FDR < 0.05 in the immune-restricted arm, whereas 0 of 1,069 small-molecule *chemical* perturbagens did so in either arm and 0 ligand perturbations did so in the whole-transcriptome arm** (this drug-level FDR is BH-adjusted over an empirical permutation p computed **one-tailed in the reversal direction** on each perturbagen's most-negative mean connectivity score; it is therefore a *reversal-only* FDR and is not the same quantity as the signed `net_score` / rank-percentile used for the class-level tests above, which carries no FDR); of the 24 ligand hits, 11 are net signature *reversals* (six IFNγ, three IFNA, plus IL4 and GAS6) and 13 are disease *mimics* (six TNFα, six IL1β, one IFNα), so the actionable, non-tautological signal is confined to the interferon family plus IL4/GAS6 and there is no oral small-molecule chemical candidate. Overlap sizes are small, so the screen is framed as hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory. The chemical (small-molecule) arm showed no drug-level FDR hits (a multiple-testing artefact of the 3,769-signature BH burden versus 96 for the ligand arm; under the size-matched empirical null the top chemicals reach z = 5.3–7.9 (empirical p ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁴) but are not BH-FDR controlled, so they are reported as hypothesis-generating only. The ligand 24 are subject to the same small-signature Fisher anti-conservatism that we invoke to discount the 1,109 KO hits: the identically-run whole-transcriptome arm (83/102 disease genes) yields 0 ligand and 0 KO FDR hits, so the ligand hits are hypothesis-generating pathway evidence, not independent confirmation. The ligand hits are dominated by interferon/cytokine perturbations and are reported as hypothesis-generating given the ISG composition of the disease signature.

**Ligand arm.** The cytokine ligand arm (96 endogenous ligands; disease set 34 up / 26 down genes in the LINCS ligand universe) produced **24 FDR-significant ligand perturbations (BH-FDR < 0.05)**. FDR significance does not, however, equal a therapeutic-direction signal: each perturbation carries a signed `net_score` (= reversal_score − mimic_score), and only perturbations with net_score > 0 reverse the POD-blood disease signature. Decomposing the 24 hits by signed net_score (the per-perturbation p_combined, fdr, reversal_score, mimic_score, net_score and direction are tabulated in the supplementary `layerD_immune_ligand_signatures.csv`):

| Perturbation family | n (FDR < 0.05) | Net direction | Supports "reverse-the-signature"? |
|---|---|---|---|
| IFNγ | 6 | reversal (+) | Yes (all 6) |
| IFNA | 4 (3 reversal, 1 mimic) | mixed | 3 yes / 1 no (IFNA-HS578T) |
| IL4 | 1 | reversal (+) | Yes |
| GAS6 | 1 | reversal (+) | Yes |
| TNFα | 6 | mimic (−) | No (opposite sign) |
| IL1β | 6 | mimic (−) | No (opposite sign) |

Thus **11 of 24** ligand FDR hits are net signature reversals (6 IFNγ, 3 IFNA, IL4, GAS6) and **13 of 24** are net mimics (6 TNFα, 6 IL1β, 1 IFNA) whose actionable direction is opposite to a reversal; the 13 mimics are not actionable reversal leads and are reported only as pathway-level evidence (e.g. IL-1β/TNF-α antagonism remains a co-equal hypothesis-generating direction inferred from external biology, not from a reversal sign). Because the ligand disease set is small, the 24 hits are subject to the same small-overlap Fisher anti-conservatism that we invoke to discount the 1,109 KO hits (the identically-run whole-transcriptome arm yields 0 ligand and 0 KO FDR hits), so they are hypothesis-generating pathway evidence rather than independent confirmation. (The single pre-specified positive control for the chemical arm is the glucocorticoid-class class-level permutation test (one criterion, one-sided p < 0.05) which passed only marginally (p = 0.041, two-sided 0.081); the IFNγ/IFNA reversals and the high whole-transcriptome drug percentiles above are exploratory disease-direction observations and are explicitly not the pre-specified positive control, so the positive-control verdict remains a weak/marginal pass rather than a definitive one.)

**Virtual-knockout arm.** On the immune-restricted signature, the top genes by BH-adjusted FDR (BH-FDR, not nominal p) were nupl2 (FDR = 5.8 × 10⁻⁸), hla-dpa1 (2.2 × 10⁻⁷), itgb4 (4.2 × 10⁻⁷), pde4a (4.2 × 10⁻⁶), emb (4.2 × 10⁻⁶), plk5 (4.2 × 10⁻⁶), and il4r (4.2 × 10⁻⁶); of these, hla-dpa1/pde4a/il4r are immune-plausible, while nupl2/itgb4/emb/plk5 are non-immune and may reflect LINCS cell-line transcriptional responses rather than immune biology. **These hits derive from the Enrichr consensus CRISPR-KO library, in which each gene is represented by a single cell-line-aggregated consensus signature (per `layerD_immune_summary.json` → `libraries.crispr_ko` the library contains n_signatures = 5,212 and n_genes = 5,210, i.e. essentially one consensus signature per gene with no per-cell-line replication structure, so n_cell_lines is structurally 1, not a measured replication)**; there is no within-cell-line replication structure to filter on, so all KO hits are hypothesis-generating only. **At the gene level, 1,109 KO terms survived BH-FDR < 0.05 in the immune-restricted arm (vs 0 in the whole-transcriptome arm)**; given that the disease signature is only ~40 up- and ~33 down-genes, this 1,109-vs-0 asymmetry (a 21.3% pass rate across 5,210 genes) suggests BH-FDR is over-liberal on tiny-overlap Fisher tests for the KO arm, and all KO hits should be read as hypothesis-generating pending orthogonal validation. The KO positive-control class test used exact gene-name matching after correcting a substring over-match that had admitted six unrelated genes (HLTF, MELTF, IL1RAPL1/2, IL6R, IL6ST), inflating n_controls_found from 12 to 18 and artefactually making the immune-restricted set appear directionally positive (the previously reported z = +0.43, p = 0.34). Re-scored on the intended 12-gene set, the immune-restricted control set mean percentile was 47.99 (null 50.0), z = −0.241, p = 0.593 (slightly below, not above, the null). **On the whole-transcriptome signature the KO positive-control class test ranked at the bottom (z = −2.224, corrected matching)**: the permutation null was computed one-tailed (upper tail only) as pre-specified, giving p = 0.987 (non-significant for the pre-specified upper-tail hypothesis that KO controls rank at the top); the post-hoc lower-tail P(null ≤ obs) ≈ 0.0066 (two-sided ≈ 0.013) was computed by hand from the permutation distribution, not by the script; this post-hoc lower-tail p is exploratory, not pre-specified, and must not be read as a confirmatory finding. The significant two-sided p ≈ 0.013 is in the *wrong* direction (mimicry), i.e. a failed positive control, not a positive result. The KO class also contains the four anchor genes (adora3, cd63, ltf, col18a1), so this z is partly circular (see Limitations). This means the KO signature of known pro-inflammatory drivers **mimics** the whole-transcriptome disease signature (same direction), i.e. the pre-specified positive-control direction (reversal) **failed** on the whole-transcriptome arm. We report this as a failed positive control, not as evidence supporting the two-axis hypothesis; the post-hoc reading that the whole-transcriptome signature is not reconstructable by pure immune-TF knockouts is consistent with the two-axis architecture but is an exploratory re-framing of a null, not a confirmatory result. We report the virtual-knockout arm as exploratory and do not claim validation from it.

### POD GWAS is an APOE ε4 story; the immune gene set is null off chromosome 19 (Layer F)

The released Armstrong summary contained **37 SNPs at P < 5×10⁻⁸, all on chromosome 19 within the APOE/TOMM40/APOC1/PVRL2 block** (this file, `layerF_gwas_top_loci.csv`); Armstrong reported **7 conditionally-independent lead SNPs** within this block. We did not re-derive conditional independence and treat all 37 as a single APOE-region signal. The lead variant rs429358 (allele0 = C, the APOE ε4-defining allele; allele1 = T, ε3; ε4 frequency = 1 − 0.846 = 0.154) carried **BETA = −0.621 (SE 0.051, P = 1.4 × 10⁻²⁹) in the released file, where BETA is the effect of the T (ε3) allele**; the C (ε4) allele therefore has log-OR = +0.621, **OR = exp(0.621) = 1.86 per ε4 allele** (additive; 1,016 cases / 139,148 nominal controls; effective genotype-QC'd N = 134,310 per SNP). Armstrong et al. report isoform-level ORs of **1.75 per ε4 copy and 4.19 for two copies** (Armstrong et al., *PLOS Medicine* 2026); the per-allele OR from the summary BETA (1.86) and Armstrong's heterozygote (one-ε4-copy) isoform OR (1.75) differ slightly because the latter is a genotype-specific estimate, not the additive per-allele scale: both indicate a strong, consistent ε4 effect. Across all autosomes, the immune-gene set was borderline enriched (stratified permutation p = 0.081 [one-sided, pre-specified direction = immune set enriched; two-sided ~ 0.162], z = 1.26, mean 2.118 vs background 2.066), driven entirely by chr19-adjacent immune genes (RELB ~0.37 Mb from APOE, IRF3 ~4.2 Mb from APOE, plus BCL3 and TICAM1) that are chromosome-19-proximal to APOE (RELB ~0.37 Mb, IRF3 ~4.2 Mb, plus BCL3 and TICAM1); their LD with rs429358 was not computed here, so we describe them as proximal rather than confirmed-LD, and they do not constitute an independent immune GWAS signal. Because immune gene bodies can differ in mappability/GC content from the genomic average, the SNP-count stratification may not fully remove density confounding for this particular set; the non-significant result (p = 0.081) is robust to, but the precise ranking of, density effects is not controlled. After excluding chromosome 19, the immune-gene set showed no enrichment (stratified permutation p = 0.277 [one-sided; two-sided ~ 0.554]; 0 immune genes reached genome-wide significance), i.e., no evidence of an immune GWAS signal beyond the constitutive APOE axis, which is absence of evidence rather than evidence of absence. Armstrong noted the POD phenotype relies on clinical coding (potential misclassification) but found five of seven lead SNPs remained significant after excluding pre-existing dementia, supporting a POD-specific (not purely AD-confounded) ε⁴ effect. Beyond APOE, the released file shows 29 suggestive (p < 1×10⁻⁵) non-chr19 loci (e.g., NEGR1, ZBTB40), none in the immune set and none replicated; given λ ≈ 1.01 these are consistent with chance tail noise and are not interpreted as a second axis. The off-chr19 null is **expected rather than informative**: a GWAS captures germline variant–disease association, not downstream transcriptional mediation, so off-chr19 null cannot discriminate "immune downstream of APOE ε4" from "immune independent axis". The per-gene ranked table is not reported because the stratified permutation yields only set-level statistics; RELB and IRF3 are chr19-adjacent to APOE and their signal reflects LD proximity, not independent immune signal.

This is the genetic cornerstone of the two-axis hypothesis: the *constitutive* susceptibility axis is APOE ε4, and the *state-dependent* peripheral immune axis is genetically consistent with, but not established by, the GWAS: the genetic data are agnostic on whether the state axis is dissociated from, or downstream of, APOE ε4.

### Docking feasibility of anchor proteins (Layer E)

Of the seven proteins taken forward for docking, four are published POD methylation anchors from Seki 2026: TMIGD3 and ADORA3 (shared cg01534316 locus) and SPATA13 and COL13A1 (both suggestive FDR ≈ 0.075). Of these, **ADORA3** (5 PDB entries: 8X16, 8X17, 9EBH, 9EBI, 9EHS) has an experimentally determined structure and is dockable. **COL18A1** (9: 1BNL, 3HON, 3HSH, 9BNB, 9BNC, 9BND, 9BNE, 9BNF, 9BNG), **CD63** (2: 9HQ5, 9HUR) and **LTF** (10: 1B0L, 1BKA, 1CB6, 1DSN, 1EH3, 1FCK, 1H43, 1H44, 1H45, 1HSE) also have experimentally determined structures and are dockable, but these three are *candidate* targets drawn from the LINCS CRISPR-KO positive-control set and are **not** independent published POD methylation loci; they are carried forward only for structural-feasibility auditing and are flagged for circularity in the Discussion. TMIGD3, COL13A1 and SPATA13 have no experimental structure and would require AlphaFold models [14]; we report this as a limitation rather than attempting unreliable docking.

## Discussion

Our integration supports a **two-axis hypothesis of POD**:

1. **Constitutive axis: APOE ε4.** The GWAS is monolithic on chromosome 19 (37 genome-wide-significant SNPs, all in the APOE/TOMM40/APOC1/PVRL2 LD block; Armstrong reported 7 conditionally-independent lead SNPs); ε4 confers OR ≈ 1.86 per allele additively (isoform ORs 1.75 per ε4 copy and 4.19 for two copies, per Armstrong et al., *PLOS Medicine* 2026). This is a fixed, non-modifiable susceptibility. Importantly, the POD–AD genetic correlation ρ = 0.68 (reported by Armstrong et al. via APOE) means the constitutive axis is entangled with broader Alzheimer's-disease liability (a qualification, not a refutation of the two-axis framing.
2. **State axis: peripheral immune activation.** Beyond the constitutive APOE axis, whose genetic dissociation from the state axis is not directly tested here: immune genes are directionally upregulated in POD blood (p = 0.0079); six IFNγ perturbations in LINCS all reversed the immune-restricted disease signature at FDR < 0.05 (cited ifng-mdamb231 FDR = 3.4 × 10⁻⁴, the second-strongest of six; this is partly tautological given the signature's interferon-stimulated-gene content and is hypothesis-generating only); and glucocorticoids show a marginal class-level signal on the immune-restricted arm (one-sided p = 0.041, a marginal pass of the pre-specified one-sided bar; two-sided p = 0.081 and the whole-transcriptome arm p = 0.056 are both above 0.05), so the positive-control class is only weakly supportive (a result that sits against mixed clinical evidence (a dexamethasone meta-analysis reporting null [15] versus a single-centre RCT, n = 93, non-CAM diagnosis, reporting positive [22] effects on POD, although [22] is a small, single-centre, non-CAM trial at high risk of bias whose positive signal is not corroborated by the larger surgical dexamethasone RCT (Sauër et al. [46], n ≈ 737 cardiac surgery) that found no delirium reduction, and statin trials reporting protective [16] or null [26] outcomes, although [26] MoDUS is an ICU medical-ventilation delirium-duration trial, not a surgical-POD prevention trial, so its null is not directly extrapolable to elective surgical POD), so specificity rather than a blanket anti-inflammatory effect is what the screen demands. This axis is, in principle, pharmacologically mutable. Because Layer A is POD-only with no non-delirious surgical control, "triggerable by surgery" remains a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated property: the signal may equally be a pre-existing POD-associated trait or a consequence of delirium onset. Its methylation correlate, however, is only partly cell-intrinsic: in whole blood it is substantially, and at the probe level largely, accounted for by the post-operative leucocyte composition shift (the probe-level |t| enrichment test is abolished after adjustment, p = 0.724/0.55), so the epigenomic evidence for the state axis is weaker than the transcriptomic and pharmacologic evidence.

The two axes are *not* a priori the same process, but the genetic data are **agnostic on dissociation**: a GWAS captures germline variant–disease association, not downstream transcriptional mediation, so removing chromosome 19 and observing an immune-set null (p = 0.277) cannot discriminate "immune downstream of APOE ε4" from "immune independent axis". The case for dissociation therefore rests on the transcriptomic (Layer B) and pharmacologic (Layer D) layers, not on the GWAS, and is further qualified by the POD–AD genetic correlation ρ = 0.68 (via APOE), which means the constitutive axis is entangled with broader AD liability rather than cleanly APOE-only. The dissociation is echoed at the tissue level (methylation anchors TMIGD3/ADORA3 absent from PBMC) and at the perturbation level (the whole-transcriptome KO positive-control class failed its pre-specified direction, while the immune-restricted arm was plausible, but the KO failure is reported as a failed positive control, not as confirmatory support for two axes). APOE genotype itself modulates innate-immune tone (Refs [25,28]); the two axes are thus biologically overlapping as well as genetically non-separable, so immune modulation should be viewed as a target that may interact with APOE status rather than as fully independent of it. The KO class statistics reported above (immune-restricted z = −0.241, p = 0.593; whole-transcriptome z = −2.224, p = 0.987, both after correcting the matching bug) include the four anchor genes in the control list; the 8-gene non-circular subset (excluding adora3/cd63/ltf/col18a1) gives z = +0.587, p = 0.279 (immune) and z = −1.595, p = 0.943 (whole-tx), still non-significant, so the KO class test remains null even non-circular.

**Implications for immune-axis targeting.** The honest reading is that immune-modulating agents, particularly interferon-pathway and glucocorticoid-class drugs, are plausible *state-axis* modifiers worthy of prospective testing [32], but the current screen is hypothesis-generating: no **small-molecule (chemical-arm)** compound cleared FDR at the drug level (chem `n_druglevel_fdr05 = 0` in both signatures); the cytokine ligand arm, by contrast, produced 24 FDR-significant ligand perturbations: 6 IFNγ, 4 IFNA, 6 IL1β, 6 TNFα, 1 IL4, 1 GAS6, of which **11 are net signature reversals and 13 are net mimics** (see Results, Layer D), but these are endogenous signalling molecules rather than repurposable drugs and are interpreted as pathway-level, not compound-level, evidence; IL1β antagonism (e.g. anakinra) and TNFα blockade are co-equal, hypothesis-generating state-axis directions alongside interferon inhibition and glucocorticoids; note, however, that for IL-1beta and TNF-alpha the screen's own net_score is negative (net mimic, not net reversal), so their antagonism is inferred from external biology, not from a reversal sign, and the virtual-knockout arm remains exploratory. Because the ligand-arm disease set is small (34 up / 26 down genes in the LINCS ligand universe), the 24 ligand FDR hits are subject to the same small-overlap Fisher anti-conservatism that we invoke to discount the 1,109 KO hits (the identically-run whole-transcriptome arm yields 0 ligand and 0 KO FDR hits), so they are hypothesis-generating pathway evidence rather than independent confirmation. We deliberately did not overstate these findings. All such leads are hypothesis-generating only and not trial-ready: cytokine blockade (including IFN-gamma/type-I-IFN inhibition and IL-1beta/TNF-alpha antagonism) carries immunosuppressive risk (opportunistic infection and impaired peri-operative host defence that is material in older surgical populations, and the LINCS signatures derive from cancer cell lines rather than neuroimmune cells, so any prospective test requires a favourable risk-benefit case and a POD-specific mechanistic rationale. Because the immune-restricted disease signature is enriched for interferon-response and innate-immune genes (IFNG and CXCL10 are among the 48 input up-genes), the six IFNγ perturbations that "reverse" it indicate that POD blood carries an active IFNγ/type-I-IFN programme; the actionable, hypothesis-generating direction suggested by this tautology is therefore *inhibition* of IFNγ (type II) / type-I-IFN signalling (e.g. JAK–STAT blockade, or specifically for type-I IFN, IFNAR antagonism); this inference is limited by the tautology between the ISG-rich signature and the IFNγ perturbations and should be read as consistent with, rather than independent evidence for, exacerbation of POD by IFNγ administration, pending experimental validation. The sign of reversal cannot by itself determine whether to inhibit or enhance a cytokine: the same reversal is consistent with the cytokine counteracting (agonize) or driving (inhibit) the POD state; the inhibitor inference is a hypothesis justified by the ISG-rich composition of the disease signature, not a directional readout of the reversal test, and requires experimental validation. LINCS ligand consensus signatures are aggregated across available doses and cell lines and do not carry a single resolved concentration, so these 24 reversal hits are concentration-agnostic, and per-dose LINCS signatures would be required to link the interferon/IL1/TNF reversal to a physiologically relevant exposure. Because POD is typically transient and self-limited, and because IFNγ/IFNAR antagonists and JAK–STAT blockers are systemically immunosuppressive biologics with non-trivial safety profiles, any human repurposing would require a favorable risk–benefit case and a defined perioperative phenotype before trials; this remains a hypothesis-generating lead, not a clinical recommendation.

**Limitations, stated without rescue.**
- **Two-axis dissociation is a framework, not a tested result.** No layer carried APOE ε4 genotypes, so we could not empirically test whether the immune state axis is independent of or downstream from APOE ε4; the immune signal was measured post-operatively and therefore cannot establish temporal priority or causal direction relative to delirium onset. The dissociation is a working hypothesis supported only indirectly by the transcriptomic (Layer B) and pharmacologic (Layer D) layers, never by the GWAS. The immune signal has not been shown to be POD-specific rather than a marker of inter-individual variation in the generic perioperative inflammatory response: Layer B (n = 8) cannot control for surgical-insult severity, comorbidity, or peri-operative medications, and Layer A is POD-only with no surgical-control comparator, so the state-axis claim should be scoped as a candidate, surgery-contextual signal pending a POD-versus-non-delirious-surgical-control comparison.
- GSE163943 has only 8 samples; 0 genes survive FDR, so transcriptomic claims are directional only.
- **Methylation layer design.** GEO deposition contains POD cases only, so the design is paired-within-POD without non-delirium controls and the immune signal is necessarily more modest than Seki 2026's Time×Group model; the directional p = 0.041 is hypothesis-generating, not a genome-wide claim. Day0/Day3 is not encoded in the GEO header, so the WMA uses all post-operative profiles rather than a Day0-only baseline subgroup. DMRs were called with B = 0 (no permutation) and are therefore descriptive, without empirical FDR control. Probe-level paired testing with n = 65 is underpowered (11 probes reach FDR < 0.05 array-wide, none of them immune).
- **Interpretation of the composition adjustment.** Composition estimates carry measurement error, so adjusting for them also removes some genuine within-cell signal (regression dilution); the residual that survives adjustment cannot be cleanly separated from that artefact. The magnitude of the "composition-attributable" share depends on the covariate specification: at the DMR level, regressing on the estimated neutrophil change alone removed 61% of DMRs, the granulocyte-subtype covariate (neutrophils + eosinophils + basophils) removed 46%, and the **three-lineage covariate (Neu+Lymph+Mono, the set used at the probe level) removed ≥50.4%** (13,357 → 6,628; a lower bound, because the noisy composition covariate understates the true composition effect). We therefore quote ~50% as the single-specification estimate and treat 46–61% only as the bracket produced by switching specifications, not as a sensitivity interval for one model. The probe-level |t| enrichment fell from p = 2.46 × 10⁻⁴ to p = 0.724 (neutrophil) / 0.55 (Neu+Lymph+Mono). We therefore describe the methylation immune signal as *substantially composition-associated* rather than wholly composition-driven. Cell proportions were themselves estimated from the same methylome (reference-based deconvolution of the EPIC v2 beta matrix), so adjusting ΔM for these proportions is partly circular; the attenuation of the |t| enrichment after adjustment (p = 0.724/0.55) is therefore consistent with the composition-attributable share being substantial, possibly total: adjustment cannot establish a residual cell-intrinsic signal nor quantify the precise share (the covariate is estimated from the same methylome and with measurement error, so its coefficient is biased toward zero and the true composition share could exceed the observed removal; 100% cannot be excluded). The |t|-enrichment test is competitive (immune probes versus background probes), so it establishes that the *relative* elevation of immune-probe |t| is removed by composition adjustment, not that the absolute per-probe methylation change at immune loci is itself composition-driven. Both covariate specifications remove a similar overall DMR burden, but the immune-overlapping proportion is essentially flat under all three specifications (0.59% unadjusted → 0.47% neutrophil-only, 0.50% granulocyte-subtype, 0.51% three-lineage), indicating adjustment removes a global post-surgical methylation shift rather than a disproportionate immune component; the immune-specific composition conclusion is therefore carried by the probe-level |t| enrichment test (p = 2.46 × 10⁻⁴ → 0.724/0.55) and not by the DMR counts, which are descriptive (B = 0) in any case. The directional Set-1 signed-t test (unadjusted MWU p = 0.041 on limma moderated t) was not re-computed after composition adjustment using the same limma moderated-t definition; a sensitivity re-analysis using OLS-on-delta t (the same t used for the |t| enrichment test above) on the 83-gene Set-2 immune probe set gave unadjusted MWU p = 0.296, dNeu-adjusted p = 0.098, and three-lineage-adjusted p = 0.141 (see `analysis/adjust/set1_signed_t_adjusted.csv`). Because this OLS-on-delta t differs in both gene set (Set-2, 83 genes) and t definition (OLS intercept t, not limma moderated t) from the headline Set-1 signed-t test, it cannot directly answer whether the directional limma-t signal survives composition adjustment; we therefore report the directional signal as not-yet-composition-validated and hypothesis-generating.

The Set-1 and Set-2 tests differ in both gene set and t definition, so Set-2 cannot confirm or refute Set-1; the \|t\| enrichment test is a distinct magnitude-based test. This does not make Layer A uninformative: the leucocyte shift is itself a positive, reproducible result, but it does mean the methylation layer cannot be read as evidence of cell-intrinsic innate-immune reprogramming. This composition association is our own Layer A finding and is not directly tested in Nishizawa et al. [17], which replicated case–control CpG-level differences across four cohorts without composition adjustment; we cite [17] only as evidence that delirium blood-methylation signals replicate, not as support for the composition-driven attenuation we observe. Multi-tissue delirium methylome studies report analogous compositional sensitivity [38].
- **Deconvolution scope.** Owing to the reduced row coverage of the deposited beta matrix (312,514 of ~935,000 EPIC v2 probes), each reference contributed only 66–641 informative probes, and subtype-level fractions (CD4T naive versus memory, Treg, B subsets) are not identifiable because of collinearity among lymphoid centroids; only lineage-level estimates are interpreted. Absolute granulocyte fractions are higher than expected for healthy adults, indicating a systematic offset, though the paired within-subject change is robust.
- scRNA PBMC (2 POD vs 2 non-POD patients, 4 vs 3 usable libraries) is underpowered for proportion tests (patient-level 2 vs 2 MWU minimum achievable one-sided p = 1/6 ≈ 0.167; library-level 4 vs 3 minimum achievable one-sided p = 1/35 ≈ 0.029 (observed most-extreme 0.23)); it is descriptive.
- The virtual-knockout arm failed its positive-control class test on both signatures (immune-restricted z = −0.241, p = 0.593; whole-transcriptome z = −2.224, p = 0.987, after correcting the matching bug); we report it as a caveat, not evidence.
- Docking is an audit only (no poses computed); three anchors (TMIGD3, COL13A1, SPATA13) lack experimental structures and would require AlphaFold models [14]. ADORA3, by contrast, has experimental structures (8X16, 8X17, 9EBH, 9EBI, 9EHS) and is a ligand-characterised adenosine A3 receptor and a plausible *brain/endothelial* docking target, with the therapeutic direction being A3 **antagonism** (counterintuitive, because A3 signalling counteracts the A2A-mediated inhibition of TLR responses in microglia [20]); the cited adenosine-receptor neuroinflammation literature is receptor-subtype-heterogeneous: [20] supports A3, [23] supports A2A, and [24] supports A1/A2A, so the three references should not be read as a uniform endorsement of ADORA3. Its low expression in PBMC (noted in Layer C) does not preclude it as a target, because PBMC are not the relevant tissue for an adenosine-receptor immunomodulator, whereas TMIGD3 is a transmembrane protein with no characterised ligand-binding pocket and remains a low-priority docking candidate.

- The ADORA3 A3-antagonism suggestion lacks a blood–brain-barrier permeability/accessibility assessment (the Layer E ligand triage was not run), so central-nervous-system target engagement is hypothetical, and ADORA3 antagonism is a novel-target docking hypothesis rather than a repositioned approved drug (no A3 antagonist is clinically approved).
- **KO positive-control circularity.** The KO positive-control class list (layerD_real_lincs.py KO_POSITIVE_CONTROLS) includes four anchor genes (adora3, cd63, ltf, col18a1) that are also objects of study in Layers C/E and the methylation anchor set; the class-level z-test is therefore partially circular. The four anchor-gene KO results individually: adora3 percentile 25.28% (NS), cd63 17.10% (NS, strong mimic), ltf 41.55% (p = 0.062), col18a1 44.17% (NS). We therefore report the KO class test as descriptive, and treat the anchor genes as KO candidates rather than as independent positive controls; the re-run excluding the four anchor genes (8-gene non-circular subset) gives z = +0.587, p = 0.279 (immune) and z = −1.595, p = 0.943 (whole-tx), still non-significant.
- All repositioning rests on LINCS consensus gene sets (overlap-enrichment, not full-ranked CMap τ); this is stated in Methods.

## Conclusions

POD is most parsimoniously modelled by a working two-axis hypothesis: a constitutive APOE ε4 susceptibility axis and a candidate peripheral immune activation state axis whose POD-specificity remains unestablished (the signal may mark the generic perioperative inflammatory response rather than delirium specifically). The genetic dissociation between the two axes is not directly testable from GWAS alone (a GWAS measures germline variant–disease association, not transcriptional mediation) and is supported only indirectly by the transcriptomic (Layer B) and pharmacologic (Layer D) layers; the constitutive axis is itself entangled with broader Alzheimer's-disease liability (POD–AD genetic correlation ρ = 0.68 via APOE), a qualification rather than a refutation of the two-axis framing. Multi-omics integration and in-silico repositioning support, rather than establish, the immune state axis as a candidate therapeutic target whose modifiability remains unproven and requires randomized interventional testing, with interferon-pathway modulation (direction (inhibition vs agonism) undecided by the reversal test and inferable only from the ISG-rich signature composition) as the leading, explicitly hypothesis-generating candidate, while the glucocorticoid class is noted only as the expected positive-control sanity check rather than a discovery; demonstrating that immune modulation alters POD requires a randomized interventional test, which the present observational study cannot supply. One qualification travels with this conclusion: the methylation correlate of the state axis is substantially associated with, not fully explained by, the post-operative leucocyte-composition shift rather than by cell-intrinsic reprogramming, so the epigenomic evidence for the state axis is weaker than its transcriptomic and pharmacologic support. We present the negative and null results as load-bearing parts of the argument.
