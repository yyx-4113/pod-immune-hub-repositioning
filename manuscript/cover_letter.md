**Cover Letter**

*To the Editor-in-Chief, Journal of Neuroinflammation*

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider the manuscript entitled **"A two-axis hypothesis for postoperative delirium: APOE ε4 constitutive susceptibility and a peripheral immune state axis supported by multi-omics integration, with an in-silico, hypothesis-generating drug-repositioning screen"** for publication as an Original Article in *Journal of Neuroinflammation*.

Postoperative delirium (POD) is common and morbid, but its molecular architecture is unresolved. We addressed a specific gap: whether the widely hypothesised peripheral innate-immune activation state and the well-replicated APOE ε4 genetic risk factor are the *same* axis or two dissociable axes. Using publicly available multi-omics data (blood methylome GSE330869, 65 paired POD cases, EPIC v2; blood transcriptome GSE163943; PBMC scRNA-seq GSE252572; POD GWAS, n = 134,310) integrated with an in-silico LINCS L1000 repositioning screen (chemical and CRISPR-knockout arms), we report that POD is parsimoniously modelled by a working two-axis hypothesis — a *constitutive* APOE ε4 susceptibility axis and a *state-dependent* peripheral immune activation axis — and that immunomodulatory agents plausibly target the latter, not the former; the independence (dissociation) of the two axes is a framework we could not test (no layer carried APOE ε4 genotypes) and is supported only indirectly by the transcriptomic and pharmacologic layers.

**What we believe is most distinctive here is a deliberate self-falsification step.** Whole-blood methylation differences can arise from a change in cell composition rather than from reprogramming within cells, and this is the standard objection to any peri-operative EWAS. Rather than defer it, we built it into the analysis. Reference-based deconvolution across five independent reference panels (66–641 informative probes; four constrained solvers agreeing within 1 percentage point) recovered the canonical post-operative leucocyte shift (neutrophils +3.4 percentage points, p = 7.6 × 10⁻⁴; total lymphoid −1.6 pp; monocytes −1.1 pp) — itself a positive control, since post-operative neutrophilia with relative lymphopenia is the expected surgical stress response. We then re-ran the immune tests with that estimated shift entered as a covariate. At probe level the immune-versus-background enrichment was abolished (p = 2.46 × 10⁻⁴ → 0.724 with the neutrophil covariate, 0.55 with all three lineages); at regional level, we then re-ran the DMR model with the *same* three-lineage covariate set used at probe level, which removed 50.4% of DMRs (13,357 → 6,628) and left 34 immune-overlapping DMRs; across all three specifications the immune-overlapping fraction did not rise (0.59% unadjusted → 0.47% / 0.50% / 0.51%), indicating the immune set was not disproportionately preserved. We therefore quote ~50% as the single-specification estimate and treat the 46–61% bracket only as the spread produced by switching covariate specifications, not as a sensitivity interval for one model. We therefore do *not* claim cell-intrinsic innate-immune epigenetic reprogramming: we report the methylation immune signal as substantially composition-attributable, with a modest residual regional component that adjustment does not remove. A paper that quantifies the objection and concedes the point is, we think, more useful than one that reports the unadjusted signal, and this posture matches the journal's interest in methodological rigour.

This work fits the journal's scope on peripheral immune–nervous system interactions in surgical and ageing populations. Analytic decisions and positive controls were fixed a priori (tracked in a version-controlled repository) rather than after seeing results. Null and negative findings are reported without rescue: a failed virtual-knockout class test on the whole-transcriptome signature (z = −2.47), no immune enrichment in the GWAS once chromosome 19 is excluded (p = 0.277), and no cell-type proportion shift in PBMC (minimum p = 0.229) — the last of which we explicitly reconcile with the whole-blood deconvolution result rather than leaving it as an apparent contradiction.
