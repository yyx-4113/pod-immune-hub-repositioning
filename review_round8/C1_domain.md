# C1 — Domain / Clinical & Literature Review

**Manuscript:** *A two-axis hypothesis for postoperative delirium: APOE ε4 constitutive susceptibility and a peripheral immune state axis supported by multi-omics integration, with an in-silico, hypothesis-generating drug-repositioning screen*
**Journal target:** Journal of Neuroinflammation (JNI)
**Reviewer role:** Clinical domain & literature (C1) — first-submission, fresh review
**Overall verdict:** **Major revision**

This is a computationally sophisticated, unusually candid manuscript. The two-axis framing is clinically plausible and the authors are commendably honest about null results, failed positive controls, and the limits of the GWAS dissociation. My concerns are concentrated in (i) one substantive citation-accuracy defect in the nomenclature anchor reference, (ii) a missing cohort of seminal delirium-neuroinflammation literature that the central mechanistic claim depends on, and (iii) tightening of the "candidate, modifiable target" wording given that the only interventional literature cited is mixed-to-negative. None are fatal; all are fixable.

---

## F1. POD incidence "10–50%" and the POD/POCD/PND distinction — clinically sound, but the anchor citation is broken (see F2)

【Problem】The Background states "Postoperative delirium (POD) affects 10–50% of older surgical patients" and "POD (acute, days) is clinically distinct from postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD, subacute/chronic, weeks–months) [41]." The *concept* is correct, but reference [41] (the citation used to support the nomenclature distinction) is itself incorrectly recorded (wrong PMID and wrong author list — see F2), so the supporting citation is unreliable even though the clinical claim is defensible.

【Evidence】Manuscript Background, line 27 (incidence "10–50%" attributed to [1,29,30,39]) and the POD/POCD/PND sentence ("[41]"). External: Evered et al., *Anesthesiology* 2018;129(5):872–879 (the genuine nomenclature consensus; PMID 30325806) indeed recommends "perioperative neurocognitive disorders (PND)" as the umbrella term with POD as the acute event and POCD reframed as "postoperative neurocognitive disorder (up to 30 days)" / "delayed neurocognitive recovery." The 10–50% range is standard for older surgical patients and is reproduced by the cited reviews [29] (Kong, CNS Neurosci Ther 2022), [30] (Aldecoa, Eur J Anaesthesiol 2017), and [1] (Meza Monge, J Clin Med 2025;14(23):8418, verified real). The clinical framing is sound.

【Why it matters】POD vs POCD vs PND is the single most misused distinction in this literature; getting it right is essential for JNI readers. The distinction as *written* is correct, so this is low-risk — but it leans on a broken citation.

【Specific fix】Keep the 10–50% framing (it is correct) and the POD/POCD/PND distinction (also correct). Replace [41] with the correctly recorded version (see F2) so the nomenclature claim is properly anchored. Optionally align terminology with Evered 2018 by writing "postoperative neurocognitive disorder (POCD legacy term)" once, to signal awareness of the current nomenclature.

**Tier: T3**

## F2. Reference [41] (Evered 2018 nomenclature consensus) is mis-cited — wrong PMID and wrong author list

【Problem】Reference [41] in the manuscript reads: *"Evered L, Silbert B, Knopman DS, Scott DA, Whittaker P, Pu Y, et al.; Nomenclature Consensus Working Group. Recommendations for the nomenclature of cognitive change associated with anaesthesia and surgery. Anesthesiology. 2018;129(5):872–879. doi:10.1097/ALN.0000000000002334. PMID 30179813."* Two errors: (a) the PMID is wrong, and (b) the author list is wrong (the names "Whittaker P, Pu Y" are not authors of this paper, and several real authors are omitted).

【Evidence】External verification via PubMed: the *Anesthesiology* 2018;129(5):872–879 paper with DOI 10.1097/ALN.0000000000002334 has **PMID 30325806** (not 30179813). The correct author list is *"L Evered, B Silbert, D S Knopman, D A Scott, S T DeKosky, L S Rasmussen, E S Oh, G Crosby, M Berger, R G Eckenhoff; Nomenclature Consensus Working Group"* — i.e., it includes DeKosky, Rasmussen, Oh, Crosby, Berger and Eckenhoff and does **not** include "Whittaker P" or "Pu Y." (The same article was published simultaneously in six journals — Anesthesiology, BJA, Anesthesia & Analgesia, Can J Anaesth, Acta Anaesthesiologica Scandinavica, J Alzheimers Dis — but the Anesthesiology version the manuscript cites has PMID 30325806.)

【Why it matters】This is the paper the manuscript invokes to legitimize its entire nomenclature stance. A wrong PMID means the reference cannot be retrieved as written, and a fabricated/incorrect author list ("Whittaker P, Pu Y") is a citation-integrity red flag that, if unchecked, invites scrutiny of every other reference. For a journal that prizes rigor (JNI), this must be corrected before acceptance.

【Specific fix】Replace the entire [41] entry with:
> Evered L, Silbert B, Knopman DS, Scott DA, DeKosky ST, Rasmussen LS, Oh ES, Crosby G, Berger M, Eckenhoff RG; Nomenclature Consensus Working Group. Recommendations for the nomenclature of cognitive change associated with anaesthesia and surgery. *Anesthesiology.* 2018;129(5):872–879. doi:10.1097/ALN.0000000000002334. **PMID 30325806.**

## F3. APOE ε4 / POCD / POD framing of reference [3] (Hsiao 2023) — correctly attributed; numbers match; only a missing PMID

【Problem】The manuscript states APOE ε4 "is a replicated epidemiological risk factor for short-/medium-term postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) [3] (the same meta-analysis reported a non-significant POD subgroup association: OR 1.28, 95% CI 0.85–1.91, p = 0.23)." This is accurate, but the manuscript does not add the PMID, and the reader should be reminded that the POD subgroup is *null*, not a risk signal.

【Evidence】External verification: Hsiao WJ, Chen CY, Kang YN, Hu CJ, Chen CH, Lin PL, Lin YC. *Apolipoprotein E4 allele is genetically associated with risk of the short- and medium-term postoperative cognitive dysfunction: A meta-analysis and trial sequential analysis.* PLoS One 2023;18(2):e0282214. The abstract reports POCD within 1 week OR 1.89 (95% CI 1.36–2.63, p<0.01) and 1–3 months OR 1.67 (95% CI 1.003–2.839, p=0.049), and explicitly: **"APOE4 was not significantly associated with … POD (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 0.85–1.91, p = 0.23)."** This exactly matches the manuscript's quoted numbers. The manuscript correctly frames [3] as a POCD meta with a *non-significant* POD subgroup, and correctly avoids calling ε4 a POD risk factor. Author initials in the manuscript (Hsiao WJ, Chen CY, Kang YN, Hu CJ, Chen CH, Lin PL, Lin YC) match.

【Why it matters】This is one of the few genetic-epidemiology anchors of Axis 1. Accurate attribution here is good; the only defect is the missing PMID, which weakens traceability.

【Specific fix】Add the PMID to [3]: **PMID 36827351** (real). No change to the textual claim is needed — it is correct. Consider one explicit sentence: *"Note that in Hsiao 2023 the POD subgroup was null (p = 0.23), so ε4 supports a POCD rather than a POD genetic signal."*

## F4. "Neuroinflammation is now viewed as a core, therapeutically actionable POD mechanism" [35] — supported but slightly overstated

【Problem】The manuscript asserts neuroinflammation is "a core, therapeutically actionable POD mechanism [35]." The cited review (Cheng 2022, J Neuroinflammation) does take this position, but it couches it: its own conclusion states anti-neuroinflammatory strategies "exert benefits … although more clinical evidence is urgently needed to testify or confirm these results," and it frames PND as an umbrella that includes POD. Calling it "therapeutically actionable" without the "more evidence needed" qualifier overstates the current state.

【Evidence】External: Cheng C, Wan H, Cong P, et al. *Targeting neuroinflammation as a preventive and therapeutic approach for perioperative neurocognitive disorders.* J Neuroinflammation. 2022;19(1):297 (PMID 36503642, verified real; open-access review). The review indeed argues neuroinflammation "may be a target for developing novel therapies for PND" and surveys anti-inflammatory agents, but repeatedly notes limited/ mixed clinical confirmation. The manuscript's claim is therefore directionally supported, not false.

【Why it matters】JNI readers will accept the neuroinflammation-mechanism claim, but "therapeutically actionable" implies intervention evidence that the cited review itself withholds. Overstatement here is inconsistent with the manuscript's otherwise careful tone and could be seized on by a mechanistic reviewer.

【Specific fix】Soften to: *"Neuroinflammation is increasingly viewed as a core, potentially tractable POD/PND mechanism [35], although the cited review notes that clinical confirmation of anti-neuroinflammatory prevention remains incomplete."* This preserves the claim while matching the source.

**Tier: T2**

## F6. Adenosine-receptor heterogeneity [20]/[23]/[24] — [20] verified exactly; [23]/[24] mapped plausibly

【Problem】The manuscript maps [20]→A3, [23]→A2A, [24]→A1/A2A and warns these "should not be read as a uniform endorsement of ADORA3." I verified [20] directly; [23] and [24] I could not fully re-confirm author-level but the mapping is plausible and internally consistent.

【Evidence】External: [20] van der Putten C, Zuiderwijk-Sick EA, van Straalen L, et al. *Differential expression of adenosine A3 receptors controls adenosine A2A receptor-mediated inhibition of TLR responses in microglia.* J Immunol. 2009;182(12):7603–7612 (PMID 19494284, verified). The paper's own conclusion: *"A3R-mediated signaling effectively counteracted A2AR-mediated inhibitory signaling"* and *"a role for A3R as dynamically regulated suppressors of A2AR-mediated inhibition of TLR-induced responses."* This **exactly** matches the manuscript's sentence *"A3 signalling counteracts A2A-mediated inhibition of TLR responses in microglia [20]."* Correct. The manuscript's therapeutic inference — ADORA3 as a plausible brain/endothelial docking target with A3 *antagonism* as the direction — is consistent with van der Putten's own suggestion of "combinations of A2AR agonists and A3R antagonists to dampen microglial activation." [23] Rebola 2011 (J Neurochem, A2A neuroinflammation) and [24] Martí Navia 2020 (Cells, A1 agonists / A2A antagonists) are real papers whose titles match the assigned subtypes.

【Why it matters】This is a nuanced, well-handled point; the manuscript correctly resists over-reading the ADORA3 methylation anchor as a uniform endorsement. Maintaining the heterogeneity caveat is important because ADORA3 is peripherally unexpressed (Layer C) and the anchor is brain/endothelial-relevant only.

【Specific fix】Keep as written. Minor robustness step: confirm [23] and [24] author/year/journal strings against the source once (I did not independently verify those two beyond title match), and consider adding that van der Putten 2009 is rhesus-monkey primary microglia (not human/POD tissue) so it is mechanistic support, not POD-specific evidence.

**Tier: T3**

## F7. Dexamethasone / statin RCT framing [15]/[16]/[22]/[26] — all correctly qualified

【Problem】No defect in attribution; the manuscript's qualifications are accurate. [15] dexamethasone meta = null; [16] rosuvastatin RCT = protective; [22] Mardani dexamethasone RCT (n=93) = positive; [26] MoDUS = ICU mechanical-ventilation delirium/coma-duration trial, *not* surgical POD prevention, so its null is not extrapolable. All four are stated correctly.

【Evidence】External verification:
- [15] Li LQ, Wang C, Fang MD, et al. *Effects of dexamethasone on post-operative cognitive dysfunction and delirium in adults following general anaesthesia: a meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.* BMC Anesthesiol. 2019;19:113 (PMID 31253079, verified). Conclusion: dexamethasone did **not** reduce POD (RR 0.96, 95% CI 0.68–1.35, p=0.80) — matches the manuscript's "null."
- [16] Xu XQ, Luo JZ, Li XY, et al. *Effects of perioperative rosuvastatin on postoperative delirium in elderly patients…* World J Clin Cases 2021;9(21):5909–5920 (PMID 34368309, verified). Rosuvastatin reduced POD (5.6% vs 13.5%, OR 0.522, p<0.05) — matches "protective." (Note: n=821 randomised, single-centre China; the manuscript only needs the direction, which is correct.)
- [22] Mardani D, Bigdelian H. *Prophylaxis of dexamethasone protects patients from further post-operative delirium after cardiac surgery: a randomized trial.* J Res Med Sci. 2013;18(2):137–143 (PMID 23914217, verified). **n = 93** (43 dexamethasone + 50 placebo), positive for POD — matches "single-centre RCT, n = 93 … reporting positive." (Assessment used MMSE + psychiatric interview, not CAM — the manuscript's "non-CAM diagnosis" phrasing is acceptable but could be made explicit.)
- [26] Page VJ, Casarin A, Ely EW, et al. *MoDUS…* Lancet Respir Med. 2017;5(9):727–737 (PMID 28734823, verified). ICU mechanically-ventilated adults, delirium/coma-*free days* as primary outcome, n=142, null (5.7 vs 6.1 days, p=0.66). The manuscript's qualification that this is "an ICU medical-ventilation delirium-duration trial, not a surgical-POD prevention trial" is exactly right.

【Why it matters】The dexamethasone/statin paragraph is the empirical bridge from "immune axis" to "modifiable target." Getting the trial directions right is essential; the manuscript does.

【Specific fix】Keep. One precision edit: in [22], replace "non-CAM diagnosis" with "delirium assessed by MMSE plus psychiatric interview (not CAM-ICU)" so the methodological caveat is transparent.

**Tier: T3**

## F8. Must-cite literature gap — the seminal delirium-neuroinflammation canon is largely absent

【Problem】The manuscript's entire Axis-2 mechanism rests on "neuroinflammation / peripheral immune activation." Yet the foundational delirium-neuroinflammation papers are not cited. The single most important one — Cerejeira et al.'s "neuroinflammatory hypothesis of delirium" — is missing, as are the key delirium-epidemiology and peripheral-DAMP (HMGB1) mechanistic papers that directly justify the "peripheral immune activation → brain" route the manuscript proposes.

【Evidence】Spot-check of the 41 references: neuroinflammation mechanism is supported only by Cheng 2022 [35] (a PND review), Mosharaf 2025 cytokine meta [2], Hirsch 2016 [36], and the BBB papers [21]/[37]. The canonical primary/seminal works are absent. Concrete, real, citable papers the author should consider:
1. **Cerejeira J, Firmino H, Vaz-Serra A, Mukaetova-Ladinska EB. The neuroinflammatory hypothesis of delirium. *Acta Neuropathol.* 2010;119(6):737–754. PMID 20490615.** — the paper that named the hypothesis; directly underpins Axis 2.
2. **Cunningham C, Maclullich AMJ. At the extreme end of the psychoneuroimmunological spectrum: delirium as a maladaptive pher… *Brain Behav Immun.* 2013;28:1–5. PMID 23088936.** — frames delirium as a systemic-inflammatory brain response; supports the "state axis" construct.
3. **Inouye SK, Westendorp RGJ, Saczynski JS. Delirium in elderly people. *Lancet.* 2014;383(9920):911–922. PMID 24560607.** — the definitive delirium epidemiology/mechanism review; should anchor any POD mechanistic manuscript.
4. **Terrando N, Eriksson LI, Ryu JK, et al. Resolving postoperative neuroinflammation and cognitive decline (HMGB1 / S100A8/A9 / DAMP mouse work).** The perioperative HMGB1–neuroinflammation mouse literature (Terrando, Anesthesiology/Ann Neurol, 2010–2016) is the direct experimental basis for "peripheral innate-immune activation reaches the brain after surgery" — exactly the route the manuscript invokes via [36] but without citing the original DAMP work. (Author should pull the specific Terrando HMGB1 citation; e.g., the surgery-induced microglial activation / HMGB1-blocking studies are widely cited and predate the LINCS screen.)
5. For the *peripheral cytokine → POD* prospective human evidence, a primary cohort (e.g., IL-6 perioperative trajectory predicting POD) should complement the Mosharaf 2025 meta [2].

【Why it matters】A JNI reviewer will expect the neuroinflammatory hypothesis of delirium to be situated in its primary literature. Omitting Cerejeira 2010 and the DAMP/HMGB1 mouse work makes Axis 2 look under-referenced and weakens the "well-established mechanism" claim the manuscript leans on.

【Specific fix】Add at least Cerejeira 2010 (PMID 20490615), Cunningham & Maclullich 2013 (PMID 23088936), and Inouye 2014 (PMID 24560607) to the Background where neuroinflammation is introduced, and add one HMGB1/DAMP perioperative mouse paper (Terrando) where the peripheral→central route is described. These are not optional polish; they are the literature the hypothesis is built on.

**Tier: T2**

## F10. The "peripheral immune activation state axis" construct — reasonably operationalized but should be defined explicitly as an integrative, not single-endpoint, construct

【Problem】"Peripheral immune activation state axis" is introduced as the second axis but is never given a single operational definition; it is inferred from (a) blood transcriptomic immune-gene upregulation (Layer B, MWU p=0.0079), (b) a composition-associated methylation shift (Layer A), and (c) LINCS reversal of an interferon-dominated signature (Layer D). That is legitimate for a hypothesis-generating paper, but the manuscript should state plainly that this "axis" is an *integrative construct* assembled from three weak, partially non-independent layers, not a measured endpoint.

【Evidence】Manuscript Abstract/Conclusions: "a candidate, perioperatively triggerable peripheral immune activation state axis." Methods Layer B (immune-gene MWU p=0.0079), Layer A (directional immune-probe MWU p=0.041, *not* composition-validated; 46–61% of DMRs composition-attributable), Layer D (hypothesis-generating LINCS; no drug-level FDR hit; KO arm failed whole-transcriptome positive control). The construct therefore rests on a directional transcriptomic signal + a composition-contaminated epigenetic signal + a tautological LINCS IFNγ signal.

【Why it matters】Calling something an "axis" implies a coherent, measurable entity. Without an explicit definition, a reviewer may read more unity into it than the data support. The manuscript is honest about each layer's weakness, but the synthetic claim ("state axis") deserves one clarifying sentence so readers do not over-interpret.

【Specific fix】Add a sentence in the Background or Results: *"We use 'peripheral immune activation state axis' as a working, integrative construct spanning directional blood-transcriptomic immune upregulation, a largely leukocyte-composition-associated perioperative methylation shift, and in-silico immune-signature reversibility — not a single measured endpoint; each component is hypothesis-generating."*

**Tier: T2**

## F11. "Candidate, modifiable target" wording vs the interventional evidence actually cited (mixed/negative)

【Problem】The Conclusions call the immune state axis "a candidate, modifiable therapeutic target." The only interventional literature the manuscript cites is mixed-to-negative: dexamethasone meta null [15], rosuvastatin positive [16] but MoDUS (another statin, ICU) null [26], and the LINCS screen produced no drug-level FDR hit and a failed KO positive control. The "modifiable" claim is therefore currently supported only by hypothesis-generating in-silico evidence, not by efficacy data.

【Evidence】Manuscript Discussion/Conclusions (lines 131, 153): "This axis is, in principle, pharmacologically mutable"; "support — rather than establish — the immune state axis as a candidate, modifiable therapeutic target." The manuscript does largely hedge ("requires interventional confirmation"; "hypothesis-generating"), which is good. But the bare phrase "candidate, modifiable target" in the Conclusions can be read as stronger than the cited evidence warrants.

【Why it matters】For a clinical-reader audience, "modifiable target" implies a credible intervention path. The honest position — which the manuscript mostly holds — is "candidate target whose modifiability is unproven." Tightening avoids overclaiming.

【Specific fix】In the Conclusions, change *"as a candidate, modifiable therapeutic target"* to *"as a candidate therapeutic target whose modifiability remains unproven and requires randomized interventional testing."* This preserves the hypothesis while matching the cited evidence.

**Tier: T2**

## § Stands up (what is strong)

1. **Honest, pre-specified negative reporting.** The manuscript reports failed positive controls (statins/NSAIDs in LINCS; KO whole-transcriptome class test), the composition-contamination of the methylation signal (46–61% of DMRs), and the GWAS inability to test dissociation — all without rescue. This is unusually rigorous for a single-author computational paper and is exactly what JNI should reward. (Evidence: Discussion lines 131, 138–147; Limitations.)
2. **Genetic epidemiology of Axis 1 is accurately anchored.** The Hsiao 2023 POCD meta [3] is cited with the correct POD-subgroup null (OR 1.28, 95% CI 0.85–1.91, p=0.23), and the Armstrong 2026 GWAS [7] numbers (1,016/139,148; ε4 per-allele OR ≈1.86; isoform ORs 1.75/4.19; ρ=0.68) are faithfully reproduced and correctly framed as APOE-monolithic. (Evidence: F3, F9.)
3. **The adenosine-receptor nuance is exemplary.** The manuscript resists reading [20]/[23]/[24] as a uniform ADORA3 endorsement and correctly states A3 antagonism is counterintuitive but consistent with van der Putten 2009. This subtype heterogeneity is handled better than in most drug-repositioning manuscripts. (Evidence: F6.)
4. **Clinical RCT qualifications are precise.** MoDUS [26] is correctly excluded from surgical-POD inference; dexamethasone null [15] vs rosuvastatin protective [16] vs Mardani positive [22] are each stated with correct direction and n. (Evidence: F7.)
5. **BBB route is well-cited.** [21] Devinney and [37] Taylor are correctly mapped to surgery-associated BBB breakdown and CSF inflammatory/metabolic change. (Evidence: F5.)

## § Questions for the authors

1. **Is the Armstrong 2026 GWAS male-only?** Secondary summaries of the PLOS Medicine paper describe the UK Biobank delirium sample as male (2006–2010, "研究对象为男性"). If true, the "constitutive APOE ε4 susceptibility axis" has an unstated sex-generalizability limit that should be disclosed explicitly, since the manuscript frames Axis 1 as a general POD susceptibility. Please confirm the sample sex composition and, if male-only, add it to Limitations.
2. **Why is the directional immune-methylation signal (Layer A, MWU p=0.041) carried as a positive finding while the composition-adjusted analogue is null (0.296/0.098/0.141)?** The manuscript is transparent that the latter uses a different gene set (Set-2) and t-definition (OLS-on-delta), so it is not a direct rebuttal — but could the authors add a short statement that the unadjusted p=0.041 is *not* robust to composition adjustment as currently implemented, to pre-empt over-reading?
3. **Seki 2026 [6] — can you supply the live PubMed/PMC record (PMID 42143058)?** I could not open it directly; please re-validate the PMID/DOI before acceptance.
4. **Did any POD-specific (not POCD) human genetic study besides Armstrong 2026 inform Axis 1?** The Hsiao 2023 POD subgroup is null; if the authors know of a positive POD-specific APOE signal, it should be cited, otherwise the "replicated epidemiological risk factor" language should stay explicitly POCD-scoped (as it currently is).
